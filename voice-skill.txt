MEERA PILLAI / SKINSTINCT — VOICE PROFILE
Built from: published/ (4 LinkedIn posts + 11 email newsletters)
Purpose: reference for drafting new LinkedIn posts from raw Telegram notes, in Meera's voice.

1. HOW SHE OPENS
Never a greeting-first hook on LinkedIn. She opens mid-argument with a concrete, often
counter-intuitive claim, e.g. "The niacinamide serum you're using probably has the ingredient
listed at 5% or 10% on the label. That number is almost certainly meaningless without knowing
three other things."
Newsletters open with "Hi," followed immediately by one blunt reframing sentence — she does not
warm up gradually. Founder-story pieces sometimes open with a specific dated scene ("In 2021 I
was sitting in a stability review meeting...").

2. SENTENCE LENGTH AND RHYTHM
Short, declarative sentences carry the emotional/rhetorical weight:
 - "This is legal. It is also not helpful."
 - "The actives were intact. The formulation was working as designed. It was designed for the
   wrong context."
Longer sentences are reserved for mechanism/technical explanation, and are almost always followed
by a short sentence that restates the stakes in plain language. Paragraphs are short — often 3-5
sentences — and each paragraph tends to carry exactly one idea or one data point.

3. USE OF DATA / EVIDENCE
Never a vague claim without a number attached: percentages, pH values, exact timeframes, sample
sizes ("23% of returns," "pH 3.5-6.0," "14 months," "340 profile visits in 48 hours," "71% from
cities with humidity above 70%"). She frequently self-qualifies mid-argument with a signature
"I'm not saying X — I'm saying Y" construction to pre-empt overreading her point. She cites her
own brand's data plainly, including when it's unflattering (return rates, formulation delays).

4. WHAT SHE NEVER SAYS
No wellness/marketing language: no "glow," "skin-loving," "radiant," "clean," "natural" used
uncritically. No superlatives without evidence behind them ("best," "revolutionary"). No
exclamation points. No generic email sign-offs — every newsletter ends with just "Meera," nothing
else added.

5. HOW PERSONAL SHE GETS
One specific professional anecdote per piece, used strictly in service of the argument, then
pulled back into the technical point — she doesn't linger in the personal moment:
 "It is not a particularly inspiring story. It is a documentation gap and a decision to do
 something about it."
She is unusually willing to disclose what Skinstinct does NOT have or hasn't solved yet (no
Vitamin C product after 14 months of trying, no peptide product) — this functions as a
credibility move, not a confession.

6. STRUCTURE / RECURRING DEVICES
- Numbered or sequential build-up of reasoning ("first thing... second thing... third thing" or
  "Here's why it matters" as a mid-piece pivot).
- Ends many pieces with one direct, actionable instruction to the reader ("You should ask for
  them." / "Ask what the clinical testing... involved.").
- Closes technical explanations with a short punchy line that reframes the whole piece.
- Consistently favours "documentation," "evidence," "concentration," "delivery," "stability" over
  any marketing vocabulary.

DONE-WHEN CHECK: A drafted post should sound like it's making an argument from evidence toward a
specific, slightly uncomfortable conclusion — not selling anything. If it reads like generic
skincare copy or uses hype language, it has missed her voice.
